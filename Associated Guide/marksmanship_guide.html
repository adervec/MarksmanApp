§ 01

How to use these notes

Accuracy in airsoft comes from the same handful of fundamentals every aiming sport shares: a stable platform, a consistent sight picture, and a clean trigger press, repeated the same way every time. Hardware matters less than consistency — a tuned mid‑range replica in steady hands out‑shoots an expensive one in shaky hands.

▸ Two operating principles

Dry practice builds skill. Most of your gains in trigger control and sight discipline come from slow, deliberate reps at home — see §5. Live BBs are for verifying what you built, not for learning it.

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast. Speed comes from removing wasted motion and bad reps, not from rushing. Build a perfect shot first; add speed only once each shot is still perfect.

§ 02

The fundamentals

Most accuracy problems are a breakdown in one of these. Diagnosing which is the core skill (§4).

FundamentalWhat good looks like
Stance / platformBalanced and athletic: feet about shoulder‑width, weight slightly forward, relaxed but stable. The more repeatable your platform, the more repeatable your hits.
Grip / holdHigh and firm, the same way every time. With a pistol, the support hand fills the gap and most of the steering pressure is front‑to‑back. With a longer replica, get consistent cheek weld and shoulder contact.
Sight pictureAlign the sights (or centre the dot), and put that on your aim point. With iron sights, your eye focuses on the front sight.
Trigger controlA smooth, straight‑back press that doesn't disturb the sights. The shot should almost surprise you — no snatch, no flinch.
Stability / breathingSettle the replica, let your breathing pause naturally, and release the shot in that calm window rather than fighting wobble.
Follow‑throughHold your position and sight picture for a beat after the shot. Watch the BB's flight — it tells you about your hop and your aim.
§ 03

Sight picture, hop‑up & trigger

Sight picture

With irons, line up the front and rear sights level and evenly spaced, focus on the front sight, and let the target blur slightly. With a red dot, keep both eyes open and simply place the dot. The key is the same picture every shot — inconsistency here shows up as a group that wanders.

Hop‑up — airsoft's extra variable

Hop‑up puts backspin on the BB so it flies flatter and further. It is the biggest driver of vertical point of impact in airsoft:

  • Too little hop → BBs drop early (group prints low at distance).
  • Too much hop → BBs climb/loft (group prints high, then dives).
  • Tune it so a BB flies flat and level to your practice distance, then leave it alone while you work on fundamentals.

Match your BB weight to your setup (heavier BBs are more stable in wind but need appropriate hop and power). Keep BB weight consistent within a session so you're measuring you, not the BBs.

Trigger

Press straight back, smoothly, without disturbing the sights. A gas blowback has a definite break to find and respect; an electric trigger is more of a switch, so the discipline is keeping everything else still as you press. Either way: isolate the trigger finger and don't let the hand sympathetically squeeze.

§ 04

Reading your groups

Your group is a diagnostic. Separate two questions: how tight is it (precision), and where is it centred (zero)?

What you seeLikely causeFix
Tight group, but off‑centreAim / sight / hop zero, not technique Adjust sights or aim point; tune hop for vertical. Your fundamentals are working.
Centred but scatteredConsistency: grip, trigger, or platform varying shot to shot Slow down; rebuild one fundamental at a time (§5).
Stringing lowOften anticipating the shot, or too little hop at distance Dry‑practice the trigger; re‑check hop.
Stringing high / loftingFrequently too much hop Back the hop off until flight is flat.
Horizontal spreadGrip pressure or trigger pushing left/right Even out grip; press the trigger straight back.
▸ Measure, don't guess

Photograph the target, mark each hit, and let the Marksman app compute the numbers (group size, mean radius, and how far the group centre sits from your aim). Tracking those over time turns "felt better" into evidence (§8).

§ 05

Dry practice (no BBs)

The cheapest, highest‑value practice there is: building perfect reps with no projectile in play. Do it safely.

  • Make it safe first. Remove the magazine and any gas/battery as appropriate for your replica, clear it, and point it at a safe backstop. Confirm it cannot fire. (Check your replica's manual — some gas guns shouldn't be dry‑fired without gas.)
  • Aim & hold. Build a perfect sight picture and hold it steady for a slow count; watch how little the sights move.
  • Trigger reps. Press straight back and watch the sights at the "break." If they twitch, you've found something to fix — repeat until they don't.
  • Short and frequent beats long and rare: a few focused minutes most days builds more than one marathon session.
§ 06

A simple practice loop

A repeatable session structure so each range/garden trip actually moves a number:

  1. Warm up — a few relaxed shots to settle in; check eye pro and that hop is where you left it.
  2. Pick one focus — e.g. trigger control, or grip consistency. Just one.
  3. Shoot a measured group — a fixed number of BBs at a fixed distance on a clean face, slow and deliberate.
  4. Log it — photograph and record it in Marksman so the group size and zero are captured.
  5. Review — read the group (§4), note one thing to improve, and stop while it's still good.

Keep the distance, target, and BB weight consistent across sessions so the trend line means something.

§ 07

The mental game

  • Process over outcome. Aim to execute a clean shot, not to "hit the ten." The hit is a by‑product of the process.
  • One shot at a time. The last shot is gone and the next hasn't happened. Reset, rebuild the sight picture, press.
  • A routine settles nerves. The same little pre‑shot sequence every time gives your attention somewhere to go.
  • Quit while you're ahead. End on a good group rather than grinding into fatigue and reinforcing bad reps.
§ 08

Tracking it with Marksman

The whole point of measuring is to see the trend. A quick workflow:

  1. Register your replica once: marksman tool add --name "My AEG" --category AEG --bb-mm 6.0
  2. After a group, photograph the face with each hit marked, then analyse it: marksman analyze --tool <id> --target "Airsoft Practice 10m" --distance 10 --image shot.png (or type coordinates with --shots).
  3. Watch progress over time: marksman progress --tool <id> --sessions.

Group size shrinking and the group centre creeping toward your aim point is exactly what improvement looks like on paper. See the project README for the full command reference.
